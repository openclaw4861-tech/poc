Executive Summary

This week's research confirms that AI is moving decisively from general-purpose tools toward installable expertise packages — domain-specific skills that execute tasks without requiring cloud connectivity or AI fluency from the user. For PGC, this means the window to lock in proprietary glazing knowledge as a digital asset is narrow and closing. Simultaneously, the ecosystem is waking up to the need for auditability: verifiable records of what AI did and why, which is a deployment prerequisite for field operations with safety and liability exposure. The practical takeaway: the technology is ready for targeted pilots, but only if PGC builds audit infrastructure and provenance documentation into any rollout from day one.

Top 10 Technology Trends

1. Skills as the New Unit of Value

What it is: AI capabilities packaged as discrete, executable units — not chatbots or general models, but task-specific bundles combining knowledge, logic, and workflow. WikiSkill, sepia, and PRAXIST all follow this model: a glazing-specific skill would encapsulate material properties, installation procedures, failure diagnostics, and code compliance in a deployable format.

Why it matters: This shifts the AI value proposition from "use our model" to "license our expertise." PGC has decades of accumulated glazing knowledge that exists only in people's heads. Capturing and packaging that knowledge creates a defensible digital asset before competitors do the same.

2. Auditability as a Deployment Prerequisite

What it is: Architecture patterns — notably Persona-Execution Separation — that distinguish between what a human intended, what the AI planned, and what the AI executed. The output includes a traceable record that proves accountability for every decision and action.

Why it matters: Field operations carry real liability. When an AI assists with a structural glass specification or a facade installation sequence, PGC must be able to show exactly what it recommended, on what basis, and who approved it. Audit trails are not a compliance checkbox — they are the difference between AI-assisted decisions holding up in court and creating liability exposure.

3. Edge Deployment Reaches Production Readiness

What it is: AI systems that run locally on field devices, independent of cloud connectivity. Personal-edge-proxy architectures and related frameworks eliminate the connectivity requirement that made on-site AI assistance impractical until now.

Why it matters: Job sites are often in basements, high-rises, or remote locations with unreliable connectivity. Edge deployment means PGC can deploy AI assistance on tablets or wearables at the point of work, not just in the office. This is a direct operational upgrade.

4. Autonomous Agents Cross the Demo Threshold

What it is: AI systems capable of planning and executing multi-step tasks with measurable, computer-executable outcomes — not just generating text or images, but taking actions that produce verifiable results. PRAXIST exemplifies this shift from AI as a content generator to AI as an autonomous operator.

Why it matters: The practical implication is that AI can now handle structured workflows — submitting permit forms, scheduling inspections, cross-referencing material specs against building codes — without human initiation of every step. This is not science fiction; it is current capability.

5. The Provenance Arms Race

What it is: An escalating contest between tools that embed AI-content provenance markers (C2PA, metadata labeling) and tools that strip them (watermark removers targeting Claude, Codex, and other major vendors). The watermark-remover repository explicitly targets multiple vendors simultaneously, signaling coordinated resistance to AI disclosure rather than isolated activity.

Why it matters: PGC should not assume that AI involvement in any document or specification will be self-evident or permanent. If PGC's AI tools generate project documentation, that documentation may eventually have its AI origin obscured by common tools. Build provenance documentation into skill formats and output workflows from the start, treating audit trails as robust against deliberate removal.

6. Security Hardening at the Artifact Level

What it is: Systematic red-teaming and artifact-scanning practices applied to AI outputs — not just inputs. The concern is that AI-generated code, specifications, or documents can contain subtle vulnerabilities or manipulations that propagate into production systems.

Why it matters: If PGC uses AI to generate installation sequences, material specifications, or client proposals, those outputs must be scanned before use. This is a prerequisite for production deployment, not a post-deployment concern. Any AI system handling project specifications or client data requires artifact-level security evaluation.

7. Multimodal AI for Visual Inspection

What it is: AI systems that combine image, video, text, and sensor data to assess physical conditions — cracked sealants, misaligned glazing units, thermal bridge failures, surface defects. Multimodal models process what a human inspector sees alongside contextual data like thermal imaging or structural load specs.

Why it matters: Glazing installation quality depends heavily on visual and tactile assessment. Multimodal AI running on a tablet could flag defects during installation or inspection that a human might miss, reducing callbacks and liability claims. This is a direct quality and cost lever.

8. "De-AI" Aesthetic as Emerging Market

What it is: A growing category of tools that strip AI-sounding patterns from generated text — removing the generic phrasing, excessive hedging, and formulaic structure that makes AI output recognizable. This is distinct from watermark removal; it targets the style of AI output, not its metadata.

Why it matters: Client-facing deliverables — proposals, inspection reports, project updates — need to read as authentic human communication. As AI-generated content becomes ubiquitous, clients will increasingly value — and may come to expect — outputs that feel genuine. PGC's documentation strategy should account for this appetite for authenticity, even in an AI-assisted workflow.

9. The Authenticity Premium

What it is: Market signals suggesting that genuine human expression and craft may carry more value than polished AI output. This week's most-starred GitHub repository was a personal love letter written in LaTeX — not an AI tool, not a productivity booster. It suggests the counter-movement to AI saturation is real and commercially significant.

Why it matters: For PGC, this translates to client communications, project documentation, and marketing that emphasize human judgment and craftsmanship — not just efficiency. AI should enhance PGC's expertise; it should not replace the human voice that clients hired for trust and accountability.

10. Rapid Standardization of Skill Formats

What it is: Multiple independent projects converging on similar architectures for packaging and distributing executable expertise. This convergence suggests that within the next 6–12 months, skill format standards will solidify — whoever defines or adopts them early gains a significant structural advantage.

Why it matters: The window to establish PGC's glazing expertise as a proprietary, distributable skill package is time-limited. Once standards solidify, the barrier to entry rises. Early movers in skill capture and packaging will set the benchmarks that late movers follow.

Trend Overlaps

The ten trends are not independent — they form a tightly connected system that PGC must address as a whole:

Skills + Auditability + Edge Deployment: These three form the core operational stack. PGC captures glazing expertise as a skill package (Trend 1), which runs on edge devices at the job site (Trend 3) with a full audit trail proving every AI-assisted recommendation (Trend 2). No one element works without the others.

Provenance Arms Race + Auditability: The need for audit trails (Trend 2) is amplified by the provenance arms race (Trend 5). PGC cannot rely on embedded metadata alone — audit records must be maintained in formats that survive deliberate stripping. This makes Persona-Execution Separation architecture even more critical.

Security Hardening + Autonomous Agents + Skills: Before PGC deploys autonomous agents (Trend 4) executing glazing-related tasks from skill packages (Trend 1), artifact-level security scanning (Trend 6) is a hard requirement. The combination of autonomy and domain-specific execution magnifies the risk of propagating an embedded defect into a physical installation.

Multimodal Inspection + Skills + Edge Deployment: Visual inspection AI (Trend 7) running on a field device (Trend 3) draws on packaged inspection skills (Trend 1) to provide real-time defect detection. This is the most immediate practical integration path for PGC.

Authenticity Premium + De-AI Aesthetic + Client-Facing Outputs: The demand for authentic human communication (Trends 8–9) creates a publishing and documentation workflow requirement that sits on top of the entire AI stack. AI assists the work; humans approve and shape the final output that clients see.

MVP Experiments

These experiments are designed for a 1–7 day timeframe with minimal cost. Each tests a specific hypothesis before committing to broader rollout.

MVP 1: Glazing Knowledge Mapping (Day 1–3)

Hypothesis: PGC's internal experts hold structured, captureable knowledge that could be packaged as an AI skill.

What to do: Conduct a structured 90-minute interview with one senior installer and one project manager. Use a predefined template to capture: (a) decision rules for material selection, (b) failure pattern recognition, and (c) code compliance checkpoints. Document the output in a shared document — this is the raw material for skill packaging.

Success metric: Can the documented knowledge be expressed as If/Then/Else decision rules without significant ambiguity? If yes, the capture methodology is valid. If not, that is also useful signal — it identifies where knowledge is tacit versus explicit.

What you learn: Whether PGC's expertise is structured enough to digitize, and where the capture effort needs to focus.

MVP 2: Edge AI Demo on Specification Lookup (Day 1–2)

Hypothesis: A local AI model can answer glazing specification questions without internet connectivity at acceptable accuracy.

What to do: Install a validated edge-capable AI model on a standard laptop. Load a curated reference set: OSHA glazing safety standards, one major glass manufacturer spec sheet, and three typical PGC project specs. Test 20 real-world lookup queries from recent projects. Time the responses and rate accuracy against the actual outcomes.

Success metric: Sub-5-second response time with 90%+ accuracy on material property lookups and code reference questions.

What you learn: Whether edge AI is technically viable for PGC's reference workflow, and what the integration path to field deployment looks like.

MVP 3: Audit Trail Architecture Sketch (Day 1)

Hypothesis: PGC can design a minimal viable audit trail for AI-assisted decisions using Persona-Execution Separation principles.

What to do: Using three real recent decisions from completed projects (e.g., which sealant to specify, how to handle a delayed delivery), document: (a) who made the decision, (b) what information they used, (c) what the AI would have recommended if involved, and (d) what the audit record would need to contain. Do this on paper — no software required.

Success metric: Can you produce a written record that satisfies: "Show us what this person decided, what they knew at the time, and what they would have known if AI had assisted?" If the record is incomplete, that incompleteness is the design brief for the actual audit system.

What you learn: The exact data structure and capture points needed for a compliant audit system, without building any software yet.

Strategic Implications for PGC

Competitive window is 12–18 months. The convergence on skill format standards and the rapid maturation of edge deployment means the next 12–18 months are the period when PGC can establish proprietary glazing expertise as a digital asset with defensible value. After that, either standards are set by others or competitors have moved. This is not a speculative timeline — it is based on the current velocity of independent projects moving toward convergence.

The first mover advantage is in skill capture, not tool selection. PGC does not need to choose the "right" AI platform. It needs to capture glazing expertise in a portable format before the format wars resolve. The expertise is the durable asset; the platform is interchangeable.

Audit infrastructure is not optional and cannot be retrofitted. Any AI deployment that does not include verifiable audit trails from the start creates liability exposure rather than reducing it. Build the audit layer into the architecture before the first production deployment, not after.

Client trust is the strategic moat. The market signal around authenticity suggests that PGC's clients value human accountability and expertise more than efficiency. AI should amplify what makes PGC trustworthy — deep expertise, reliable execution, clear communication — not replace it. Use AI to sharpen the expertise; keep humans accountable for the relationship.

Edge deployment changes the ROI calculation. The economics of cloud-dependent AI include ongoing connectivity costs, latency, and single points of failure. Edge deployment removes these, making AI assistance cost-effective on a per-job basis. PGC should factor this into any pilot budget — the total cost of ownership drops significantly once connectivity dependency is eliminated.
