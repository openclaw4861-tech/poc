Weekly Tech Briefing for Steve Watts, CEO

Pacific Glazing Corporation (PGC)

Executive Summary

AI agents are evolving from reactive tools into autonomous background processes that handle exceptions through structured escalation, shifting the competitive question from "reliability" to "interruption cost." Physical AI stacks—combining LiDAR, LLM-native 3D modeling, and actuation—are now technically viable for field operations like site surveys and installation verification. Critically, verification infrastructure is under active attack from automated circumvention tooling, meaning PGC's path to differentiation must build in adversarial resilience and audit-grade interpretability from day one rather than relying on behavioral accuracy alone.

Top 10 Technology Trends

1. Agent Autonomy Maturation

What it is: AI agents transitioning from human-invoked tools to autonomous background processes (daemons) that run unattended and escalate only on undecidable exceptions.

Why it matters: The competitive advantage shifts from agent capability to escalation design—who handles what, at what cost tolerance. Organizations that master interruption economics will deploy autonomous agents years before competitors waiting for "reliable" systems.

2. Physical AI Stack Convergence

What it is: Consumer-grade LiDAR, LLM-native 3D modeling (sand3d), and accessibility-API actuation combining into an assembleable stack for field deployment.

Why it matters: Automated site surveys and installation verification are now technically viable without bespoke hardware investments. This is the highest-confidence opportunity for PGC's field operations.

3. Verification Infrastructure Under Siege

What it is: Over 5,000 combined GitHub stars on repos targeting verification bypass (SheerID circumvention, subscription fraud automation, jailbreak workflows) within days of release.

Why it matters: Agent tooling industrializes circumvention the moment access controls become economically meaningful. "AI-verified installation" claims will face automated counter-tools—this is not theoretical.

4. Statefulness Displacing Stateless Chat

What it is: Memory graphs, session persistence, and meta-access patterns enabling agents with durable context across months, not sessions.

Why it matters: Agents that maintain project context (specs, site conditions, client preferences) across installation phases outperform stateless systems. PGC's project data needs structured memory graphs, not document folders.

5. Interruption Cost as Evaluation Metric

What it is: Daemon-pattern economics reframe evaluation from "failure rate" to "cost and latency of human interrupt."

Why it matters: Stop optimizing for zero-failure agents; start designing escalation workflows with quantified interruption costs per exception tier. This determines deployment timing, not agent capability.

6. Audit-Grade Interpretability Emerging

What it is: Mechanistic interpretability research including formal certification of what experiments can prove about internal computation, not just behavioral outputs.

Why it matters: "Same behavior, different mechanism" findings prove behavioral testing cannot distinguish systems differing in safety-relevant internals. Regulatory substrate is forming around interpretability documentation, not just pass/fail testing.

7. AI-Accelerated Capability Compounding

What it is: Compounding innovation cycles compressing capability windows faster, with early adoption advantages now estimated at 18-36 months and compressing.

Why it matters: First-mover windows are shorter but more decisive. Delayed adoption means competing against already-compounded capabilities, not just incremental improvements.

8. Procurement Qualification Tracking

What it is: AI-verified installation emerging as a market gate, likely mandated by late 2027.

Why it matters: Verification is becoming a procurement requirement, not just a differentiator. PGC needs verification capability now to avoid being locked out of contracts before mandates arrive.

9. Trust Infrastructure Industrialization

What it is: Verification bypass tooling transitioning from niche hacks to industrialized agent workflows.

Why it matters: Hardware-attestation requirements, device binding, and TPM anchoring will become necessary countermeasures. PGC's verification systems must architect for adversarial scrutiny from inception.

10. Documentation Automation Proliferation

What it is: Low-effort wins using meeting intelligence and cicada-style documentation capture becoming commodity capabilities.

Why it matters: Administrative overhead that once justified headcount is now automatable in days. PGC can redirect project management capacity to higher-value activities.

MVP Experiments (1-7 Days)

Experiment 1: LiDAR Site Survey Feasibility

Time: 2-3 days Cost: ~$500-800 (consumer LiDAR device rental) Steps:

  • Rent or borrow a consumer-grade LiDAR scanner (iPhone Pro with LiDAR, or handheld unit)
  • Scan one completed installation site and one active project
  • Attempt manual point-cloud generation using open-source tools (Meshroom, Open3D)
  • Evaluate whether output resolution meets minimum requirements for dimension verification

Success criteria: Point cloud captures window/frame dimensions within 1cm accuracy; workflow understood well enough to scope full pilot.

Experiment 2: Escalation Framework Mapping

Time: 1-2 days Cost: Staff time only Steps:

  • Interview two site supervisors and one project manager
  • Document the five most common exception types during installation (missing parts, measurement discrepancies, client scope changes, weather delays, access issues)
  • For each exception type, estimate: frequency per month, current handling time, and cost of current resolution path
  • Rank by interruption cost per occurrence

Success criteria: Documented exception taxonomy with cost-per-tier; clear candidate list for agent-handled vs. human-only exceptions.

Experiment 3: Project Memory Structure Audit

Time: 2-4 days Cost: Staff time only Steps:

  • Select three recent completed projects and three active projects
  • Audit where project data currently lives (email threads, PDFs, spreadsheets, drawings, verbal context)
  • Identify what data would be needed by an agent starting work on day one of each project phase
  • Map gaps between current data structure and what structured memory graphs require

Success criteria: Data structure gap analysis documenting what must change to enable agent-accessible project context; prioritized list of data capture improvements.

Strategic Implications for PGC

1. Field Operations Are the Highest-Confidence AI Opportunity

The physical AI stack is assembleable now. Site surveys and installation verification represent PGC's clearest path to AI-driven competitive advantage. LiDAR pilots should begin immediately—not to deploy, but to build institutional knowledge before competitors do.

2. Verification Capability Must Be Adversarial by Design

Verification is simultaneously a market gate and an attack target. PGC's verification systems must assume adversarial scrutiny from inception. Build for interpretability and audit trails, not just behavioral accuracy. Hardware attestation and device binding will become necessary; plan architecture accordingly.

3. Escalation Design Determines Deployment Timing

Stop waiting for "reliable" AI. The question is interruption cost, not failure rate. PGC should design escalation workflows now, defining which exceptions agents handle autonomously, which route to supervisors, and which require escalation chains. Organizations that master this deploy years ahead.

4. Procurement Mandates Are Coming—Prepare Now

AI-verified installation standards will likely become contract requirements by late 2027. PGC should build verification capability now, not as a differentiator but as a procurement qualification. Late entrants will face integration debt and contract exclusion.

5. Documentation Automation Frees Capacity for Higher-Value Work

Meeting intelligence and documentation automation are low-effort, high-return. These capabilities are commodity-level now. PGC should capture administrative wins immediately, redirecting project management capacity toward client relationship management and field supervision.

6. Data Structure Is a Strategic Asset

Agents with durable memory graphs outperform document-based systems. PGC's project data—site conditions, client preferences, specification evolution—must be structured for agent access, not human retrieval. This is a multi-year capability build, but the foundation must be laid now.

7. Early Adoption Windows Are Compressing

First-mover advantages now last 18-36 months before compression. PGC cannot afford multi-year evaluation cycles. Pilots and experiments must produce decisions—proceed or abandon—within weeks, not quarters.
