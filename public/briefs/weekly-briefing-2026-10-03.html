For: Steve Watts, CEO, Pacific Glazing Corporation Date: October 03, 2026

Executive Summary

Agent infrastructure has reached production maturity, fundamentally changing the build-versus-buy calculus for glazing automation. Small, calibrated AI models now run on commodity hardware with verifiable decision outputs, removing the enterprise procurement barriers that previously made automation risky. The convergence of these capabilities with advances in 3D generation and visual inspection creates a narrow window—approximately 18-36 months—where first-mover advantage in glazing automation remains available. The research confirms this window is closing faster than previously assessed.

Top 10 Technology Trends

1. Open-Source Agent Frameworks Reach Production Readiness

What it is: Battle-tested orchestration systems (dots, OpenDots, ZCode) that coordinate multi-step AI task execution. These frameworks handle complex workflows like planning, tool use, and memory management out of the box.

Why it matters: PGC no longer needs to build agent infrastructure from scratch. The build-versus-buy decision has flipped. A glazing-specific agent can be deployed on proven foundations, reducing development time from months to weeks.

2. Small Calibrated Decision Models for Edge Deployment

What it is: 0.8 billion parameter models (like "jeff") that deliver millisecond decisions with calibrated probability outputs on standard hardware. These models provide auditable, verifiable decision trails.

Why it matters: Addresses two PGC concerns simultaneously: data sovereignty (runs on-premise, no cloud dependency) and procurement requirements (verifiable outputs for enterprise contracts). Enables real-time decision support for field technicians without latency or security tradeoffs.

3. Skill Pack Architecture for Domain Expertise Capture

What it is: Reusable knowledge modules (universal-modder patterns) that encapsulate specialized expertise as executable skills. Each pack contains decision logic, best practices, and contextual awareness for a specific domain.

Why it matters: Direct application to glazing. PGC's accumulated knowledge—installation techniques, building codes, client preferences—becomes a deployable asset. Expert knowledge scales to every technician without requiring direct access to experts.

4. 3D Generation Capabilities Mature

What it is: Advances in Gaussian-based 3D generation, high-resolution modeling (SILSA), and automatic floorplan-to-3D conversion. Architectural visualization tools now produce photorealistic results from basic inputs.

Why it matters: Client presentations transform from static images to interactive 3D walkthroughs showing glass placement, light behavior, and aesthetic impact. Installation planning gains spatial accuracy previously requiring expensive CAD work.

5. Visual Reasoning AI for Quality Control

What it is: Multimodal models (VISTA benchmark) that solve complex inspection tasks by combining image understanding with contextual reasoning. Identifies defects, verifies standards compliance, and flags anomalies.

Why it matters: Glass quality inspection, installation verification, and damage assessment become automated. Reduces callback rates and liability exposure while standardizing quality across all projects.

6. Browser-Based AI Agents for Automated Research

What it is: AI agents with autonomous web navigation capabilities (dots framework). These agents browse, scrape, compare, and synthesize information without human intervention or blocking issues.

Why it matters: Supplier price monitoring, specification research, and compliance verification automate. Procurement research that currently takes days compresses to hours with consistent output quality.

7. Model Context Protocol (MCP) Standardization

What it is: A standardized integration layer connecting AI agents to enterprise tools and data sources. MCP enables AI systems to read from and write to existing software without custom development.

Why it matters: Integration costs drop significantly. PGC's estimation software, CRM, and project management tools connect to AI agents through a standard protocol rather than bespoke integrations.

8. Security Verification Becoming Procurement Gate

What it is: Structured benchmarks (KaliBench) testing AI tool use for security workflows. Enterprise procurement now requires audited security compliance before AI vendor approval.

Why it matters: PGC's automation systems must pass security verification to win enterprise contracts. Early adoption of verification standards positions PGC ahead of competitors facing procurement roadblocks later.

9. Multimodal AI for Site Survey Automation

What it is: Mobile-capable AI systems that process images, video, and sensor data in real-time. Guides field technicians through standardized checklists while capturing structured data.

Why it matters: Site surveys become consistent and auditable. Technician expertise requirements lower as AI prompts correct inspection sequences and captures documentation automatically.

10. Real-Time AI Video for Client Communication

What it is: Business video generation with quality standards, sound design, and motion-video-kit capabilities. Creates professional walkthroughs and presentations from static inputs.

Why it matters: Client proposals and installation explanations transform into dynamic visual content. Marketing and sales materials generate automatically from project specifications.

MVP Experiments for 1-7 Day Execution

Experiment 1: Glass Specification Extractor (2 days)

Objective: Test AI-assisted specification extraction from architectural PDFs

Setup:

  • Use Claude API or similar with a custom prompt template
  • Feed 5-10 architectural PDFs from past projects
  • Compare extraction time and accuracy against manual research

Success metric: Reduce specification research time by 50% or more on test documents

Next step if successful: Expand to full PDF library and integrate with estimation workflow

Experiment 2: Mobile Inspection Prototype (3 days)

Objective: Test multimodal AI for field documentation

Setup:

  • Use a smartphone with ChatGPT Vision or Claude mobile
  • Create a 10-item glazing inspection checklist
  • Photograph 3-5 completed installations and feed images + checklist to AI
  • Evaluate quality of structured output and issue identification

Success metric: AI captures inspection data accurately with minimal technician input

Next step if successful: Develop standardized inspection templates for field deployment

Experiment 3: Supplier Research Automation (5 days)

Objective: Test browser-based agent for procurement research

Setup:

  • Deploy dots-style browser agent (or use existing browser automation tools)
  • Define target: monitor 3-5 suppliers for price changes on top 10 materials
  • Run agent for 48 hours and compare results against manual research

Success metric: Agent identifies price opportunities or specification changes that manual research would miss

Next step if successful: Expand supplier coverage and integrate alerts into procurement workflow

Strategic Implications for PGC

1. Competitive Window Is Real and Narrow

The 18-36 month window for first-mover advantage in glazing automation is supported by current research. Open-source agent frameworks, small calibrated models, and skill pack architectures are converging now. Companies that build foundational capabilities in 2026-2027 will have sustainable advantages. Those who wait will face established competitors with trained models and integrated systems.

2. Build on Proven Foundations, Not Custom Development

PGC should leverage production-ready agent frameworks rather than building automation infrastructure from scratch. The research confirms these frameworks are battle-tested. Investment should flow toward glazing-specific skill packs and integrations, not underlying AI infrastructure.

3. On-Premise Capability Removes Procurement Barriers

Small calibrated models running on commodity hardware solve the enterprise verification problem. PGC can offer AI decision support with auditable outputs and data sovereignty guarantees—key requirements for large commercial contracts.

4. Expertise Capture Becomes Strategic Asset

The skill pack architecture pattern offers PGC a way to preserve and scale institutional knowledge. Senior glaziers' expertise becomes executable, distributed, and auditable. This is both a competitive moat and a risk mitigation strategy against workforce transitions.

5. Visual AI Applications Have Immediate ROI

Quality control automation, site survey standardization, and 3D visualization for client presentations offer tangible returns within 12 months. These applications do not require full autonomy—human oversight combined with AI assistance delivers value now.

6. Integration Strategy Determines Long-Term Success

MCP protocol adoption positions PGC to connect AI agents to existing systems without持续的 custom development. Prioritizing MCP-compatible frameworks early avoids costly integration rework later.
