Weekly Tech Briefing — November 28, 2026

To: Steve Watts, CEO, Pacific Glazing Corporation
From: Research Desk
Subject: Weekly Tech Briefing

Executive Summary

AI is fragmenting into portable, edge-run skills and high-level orchestration. Pacific Glazing Corporation can now deploy autonomous field agents on standard tablets without cloud dependency. However, GPS spoofing and adversarial AI outputs have turned raw location data and vendor files into active liabilities. Your immediate priority is to mandate agent-addressable APIs in new vendor contracts, refresh field tablets for offline inference, and budget twenty to thirty percent verification overhead on every AI-generated output before it enters production.

Top 10 Technology Trends

1. Portable Agent Skills and MCP
What it is: Model-agnostic "skills" are replacing monolithic applications, enabling multi-agent orchestration through frameworks such as OpenGame.
Why it matters: PGC can swap AI models or vendors without rebuilding workflows, but only if every new CAD, ERP, and scheduling system exposes an agent-addressable API.

2. Bare-Metal Edge Inference
What it is: Sub-1-billion parameter models now run on commodity CPUs without GPUs or cloud connectivity, demonstrated by projects such as llm.asm.
Why it matters: Field tablets can host offline safety checks and permit verification in basements, high-rises, or remote sites where connectivity is unreliable or nonexistent.

3. AI Remediation Debt
What it is: Coding and drafting agents systematically over-edit, creating hidden cleanup work that has spawned a named product category such as deslop-please.
Why it matters: Vendor quotes, shop drawings, and fabrication files generated by AI carry downstream labor costs; contracts must disclose AI generation and warrant final workmanship, not just output speed.

4. Trust Engineering
What it is: A mandatory shift from assuming AI outputs are safe to actively sandboxing and cross-verifying them, because adversarial attacks can fool safety monitors.
Why it matters: Every vendor CAD file and AI output must pass a deterministic classical cross-check before PGC commits glass, labor, or liability to it.

5. Role-Based Voice Authority
What it is: Multi-user voice agents are entering shared job sites, but the critical gap is governance over who may ask for what.
Why it matters: Without predefined authority tiers, any worker could verbally override a delivery schedule, change an order, or disable a safety hold on a voice-enabled system.

6. GPS Spoofing and Location Collapse
What it is: Hardware-level tools such as Reloc compromise iOS GPS via MFi protocols, making fleet telematics and geofenced compliance trivial to fake.
Why it matters: PGC cannot accept GPS as sole evidence of field presence; cryptographic attestation and multi-signal verification are now required for compliance and insurance.

7. Geospatial Foundation Models
What it is: Embedding models such as TerraMind turn raw site imagery into machine-readable spatial awareness for progress tracking and anomaly detection.
Why it matters: PGC can automate site surveys and catch installation deviations before they become waterproofing failures or code violations.

8. Hybrid Interpretable Safety Models
What it is: New architectures pair neural networks with decision trees to deliver black-box speed plus code-compliant, interpretable fallback logic.
Why it matters: Load and wind-resistance calculations can be accelerated by AI while preserving auditable, inspector-friendly paper trails that black-box estimators cannot provide.

9. Stack Bifurcation
What it is: Value is migrating to bare-metal inference and high-level orchestration, squeezing out bespoke middleware.
Why it matters: PGC should prefer single-binary, deterministic tools over complex integration layers that add latency, cost, and failure points.

10. Robotics Learning Acceleration
What it is: Robot training is outpacing human demonstration, compressing the timeline for useful fabrication and warehouse automation.
Why it matters: Competitors who automate glazing fabrication and material handling earlier will enjoy dropping unit costs; PGC should shorten its automation roadmap or face a margin squeeze.

Trend Overlaps

  • Edge inference and geospatial foundation models converge to create autonomous field agents that inspect sites without cloud dependency, but these agents must operate under strict voice authority rules so unauthorized commands do not trigger actions on shared job sites.
  • Portable agent skills and stack bifurcation mean PGC's procurement should demand simple, agent-addressable APIs and reject middleware-heavy platforms that cannot expose single-binary interfaces.
  • Trust engineering, AI remediation debt, and GPS spoofing form a single risk layer: every automated artifact and location signal is potentially adversarial, so sandboxed ingestion and deterministic cross-checks must become standard operating procedure before any output reaches production.
  • Hybrid interpretable models and robotics acceleration together imply that as PGC automates fabrication and installation support, safety-critical calculations need deterministic fallbacks that regulators and insurers can audit, not black-box estimates alone.

MVP Experiments

1. Offline Safety Check on a Field Tablet
Load a sub-1-billion parameter open-source model onto one standard field tablet with no GPU and no cloud connection. Run a glazing-specific safety or permit checklist against a current job file and measure accuracy and latency against your existing cloud or paper process. Goal: validate that bare-metal edge inference is viable for your worst connectivity sites within one day.

2. Sandboxed Vendor CAD Verification
Set up an isolated virtual machine or air-gapped laptop. Import a recent vendor-supplied CAD or BIM file and run deterministic classical checks, including manual dimension spot checks, file hash validation, and automated geometry rules, before the file touches your production network. Compare the time cost to your current direct-import process. Goal: quantify the overhead of trust-engineered ingestion and catch adversarial or AI-generated errors before they hit the shop floor.

3. GPS Multi-Signal Field Test
Take one company vehicle or tablet to a live job site. Record standard GPS coordinates, then capture a cryptographically hashed photo of a fixed site landmark and compare cellular tower triangulation data. Attempt to document how easily GPS alone could be spoofed versus the multi-signal approach. Goal: establish whether your current geofenced compliance data holds up to hardware-level spoofing and identify a feasible second signal for daily use.

Strategic Implications for PGC

  • Vendor Contracts: Any new CAD, ERP, or scheduling procurement must require an MCP-compatible or agent-addressable API. Vendors without this will lock PGC out of portable automation and force expensive middleware that the market is abandoning.
  • Field Operations: Refreshing tablets for offline inference removes cloud dependency for safety checks and permit verification, but IT must manage local model versions and updates. Field crews gain speed in dead zones; the tradeoff is device management.
  • Compliance and Insurance: Geofenced labor certifications and fleet logs based solely on GPS are now subvertable. PGC should expect insurers and general contractors to begin demanding cryptographic or multi-signal location attestation as standard proof-of-presence.
  • Estimating and Engineering: Hybrid interpretable models allow estimators to use AI for wind and load calculations while keeping code-compliant decision trees available for inspector review. Relying solely on black-box estimators will become a liability during permitting and litigation.
  • Cost Structure: Treat every AI-generated vendor output as a draft. Budget twenty to thirty percent review and cleanup overhead, and rewrite contracts so vendors warrant dimensional and code accuracy, not just delivery speed.
  • Workforce and Robotics: Because robot learning curves are steepening, PGC should compress automation roadmaps for the warehouse and fabrication shop. The competitive threat is not future robots; it is competitors who deploy them twelve to eighteen months earlier than expected.
  • Shared Site Governance: Before deploying voice agents in trailers or active construction zones, PGC must define role-based authority tiers. A voice command to rush a glass order or override a safety hold should require supervisor credentials, not just recognition of a spoken phrase.
