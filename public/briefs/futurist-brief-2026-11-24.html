FUTURIST TECHNOLOGY BRIEF

TO: Steve Watts, CEO, Pacific Glazing Corporation
FROM: Strategic Technology Scan
DATE: November 24, 2026
RE: Technology Signals Horizon 2029–2036

1. Horizon Summary

The dominant shift for the 2029–2036 window is a move away from general AI autonomy toward narrow, adversarial, rule-bound agentic workflows in verification-cheap B2B domains such as claims recovery and freight negotiation. Simultaneously, physical intelligence is being retrofitted onto legacy factory infrastructure through readable CAN-bus and CNC overlays rather than through greenfield automation. The critical bottleneck is no longer raw model capability but state-grounding, context freshness, and millimeter-traceable verification layers. Near-term quantum value is consolidating exclusively around classical-hybrid combinatorial optimization rather than cryptographically relevant gate-model computing.

2. Signals by Domain

Robotics and Automation

  • Trend: Retrofit tactile instrumentation on legacy factory lines. TRL 7; momentum ascending.
    Evidence: Modular tactile infrastructure is standardizing force-aware manipulation for fragile materials, and low-cost intelligence layers on existing CAN-bus and CNC equipment are now viable for repetitive, jig-bounded motions in structured fabrication environments.

  • Signal: Persistent failure of unstructured construction-site autonomy via retrofit kits. TRL 5; momentum contested.
    Evidence: The empirical base rate for "retrofit kit to full autonomy" is approximately zero; the comma/openpilot decade-long trajectory produced middleware, not full autonomy, indicating unstructured glazing installation sites remain beyond reliable automation without foundational architectural breakthroughs.

  • Signal: Metric unreliability in neural rendering for fabrication retrofit. TRL 5; momentum decelerating pending verification.
    Evidence: Gaussian Splatting produces photoreal imagery but yields metrically unreliable depth; because glazing fabrication requires millimeter-true geometry, simulation-to-reality bridges for retrofit capture are not yet trustworthy without traceable verification protocols.

Quantum and Computing

  • Trend: Local private AI inference commoditizing into enterprise plug-in appliances. TRL 8; momentum ascending rapidly.
    Evidence: Appliance form factors enabling on-premise deployment are moving from custom integration to commoditized hardware, allowing proprietary glazing specifications, contractual data, and facade-control systems to bypass cloud dependency and data-sovereignty risks.

  • Signal: Classical-hybrid quantum optimization for industrial backend problems. TRL 6; momentum ascending.
    Evidence: Near-term quantum value is migrating to Ising/QUBO solver stacks rentable as backend capacity for cutting-stock, nesting, and scheduling, distinct from cryptographically relevant gate-model computing.

  • Trend: Longitudinal back-office agents for construction deferred by stale-context architecture. TRL 5; momentum slower than projected.
    Evidence: Agents operating across 18-month construction streams involving change orders and RFIs face a stale-context problem that model scaling cannot fix, pushing viable deployment 3–5 years beyond that of narrow commerce agents.

Energy and Materials

  • Scan Result: No discrete horizon signals were flagged in the Q4 2026 scan. Existing trends in high-performance facade materials and thermal glazing remain stable without identifiable 3–10 year inflection points in the current data set.

Other Emerging

  • Trend: Narrow schema-driven B2B agentic commerce. TRL 7–8; momentum viable now.
    Evidence: Adversarial, rule-bound workflows are already operational in verification-cheap domains including insurance-claims recovery, freight negotiation, and clinic operations, demonstrating that general reasoning is not required for constrained commercial automation.

  • Signal: Context-poisoning as an adversarial attack surface in automated claims. Conceptual stage; momentum emerging.
    Evidence: Counterparty context-poisoning is identified as a real risk in adversarial claims recovery; the first context-integrity-audited claims agent is expected to capture market share in regulated trades.

  • Trend: Machine-readability via brittle extraction scraping preceding stable protocols. TRL 8; momentum stable.
    Evidence: Back-office machine-readability is arriving first through brittle extraction scraping rather than through stable, interoperable data protocols, extending integration fragility in construction document pipelines.

3. Convergence Watch

Three convergence zones deserve monitoring:

  • Retrofit Physical Automation + On-Premise Inference: Legacy fabrication lines are converging with local AI appliances to create closed-loop, force-aware glass handling in factories without greenfield robot installations. The combination of CAN-bus/CNC retrofit layers and plug-in inference boxes allows proprietary fabrication data to remain on-site while upgrading legacy equipment.

  • Adversarial Agentic Commerce + Context-Integrity Verification: Narrow B2B agents are converging with audit-trail architectures to prevent counterparty context-poisoning. In glazing, this suggests a future warranty or storm-damage negotiation ecosystem where schema-driven agents operate against insurer agents under mutually auditable, versioned state-grounding.

  • Hybrid Classical-Quantum + ERP Scheduling: Ising/QUBO solvers are converging with existing production-planning software to address combinatorial optimization problems—specifically glass nesting and cutting-stock—that exceed classical heuristic limits at large scale.

4. PGC Relevance Timeline

Near (2027–2029)

  • Deploy on-premise inference appliances for proprietary glazing specifications, contractual archives, and facade-control system data to eliminate cloud dependency and data-sovereignty exposure.
  • Pilot retrofit tactile instrumentation and readable intelligence layers on existing factory CAN-bus and CNC lines for force-aware fragile-material handling in structured fabrication environments.
  • Deploy schema-driven adversarial agents for storm-damage and warranty-claims recovery in narrow, verification-cheap workflows.
  • Defer unstructured construction-site autonomy investments; maintain human-in-the-loop for field installation robotics.

Mid (2030–2033)

  • Integrate context-integrity auditing and fresh-state grounding protocols before deploying any longitudinal agents across multi-year construction streams involving change orders, RFIs, and warranty tracking.
  • Rent hybrid classical-quantum optimization backend capacity for cutting-stock, nesting, and complex project scheduling as solver commoditization accelerates.
  • Establish millimeter-traceable verification protocols for any simulation-to-reality capture used in retrofit glazing surveys; treat unverified neural renderings as visual references only, not fabrication inputs.

Long (2034–2036)

  • Deploy fully state-grounded longitudinal agents with stale-context architecture resolved, spanning procurement through post-installation warranty across 18-month-plus construction timelines.
  • Evaluate entry into verified autonomous negotiation ecosystems for regulated glazing trades, assuming industry context-integrity standards mature.

5. One Wildcard

Unstructured Construction-Site Autonomy via Retrofit Kits.
Despite an empirical base rate of approximately zero for retrofit-kit-to-full-autonomy transitions and a decade-long middleware trajectory exemplified by comma/openpilot, a breakthrough in foundation-model grounding or multimodal state estimation could collapse the timeline for autonomous glass installation on unstructured job sites. If realized, this would eliminate the field-installation labor bottleneck and shift PGC’s value from manual site crews to fleet autonomy operations. Probability remains low, but the industry impact would be extreme.
