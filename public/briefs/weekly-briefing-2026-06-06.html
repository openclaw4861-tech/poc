Weekly Tech Briefing

To: Steve Watts, CEO — Pacific Glazing Corporation From: Technology Research Division Date: June 6, 2026

Executive Summary

The AI market is undergoing a structural shift—from speculative investment to infrastructure reliability. Self-hosted AI solutions have hit enterprise scale (58k+ stars on leading repos), agent memory systems now support persistent context across projects, and capital pressure is forcing AI vendors to offer real SLAs. For PGC, this creates a favorable window: deploy local AI without vendor lock-in, negotiate vendor contracts from a position of strength, and start with low-cost experiments that prove value before committing resources. Act now—the negotiating leverage won't last as the market stabilizes.

Top 5 Technology Trends

1. Self-Hosted AI Infrastructure Hits Enterprise Scale

What it is: AI systems that run entirely on-premise—no cloud dependency, no data leaving your network. Leading example: Odysseus (58,124 GitHub stars), a self-hosted AI workspace that handles documents, code, and workflows locally.

Why it matters for PGC: - Eliminates cloud subscription costs and data exposure risks - Gives PGC full control over AI behavior on sensitive project data - Vendor-agnostic: swap models or providers without rewriting workflows - 58k stars signals community trust and long-term stability

Bottom line: Self-hosted AI is no longer experimental. It's production-ready, and the economics favor early adoption.

2. Agent Memory Systems Reach Production Maturity

What it is: AI agents that maintain persistent context across sessions—like a colleague who "remembers" everything. Memory-OS demonstrates a 7-layer architecture: structured fact storage, auto-curated wikis, surgical context injection.

Why it matters for PGC: - Imagine an AI assistant that remembers every glazing spec, client preference, and regulatory requirement across all projects - Agents can now handle multi-step tasks (e.g., generate proposal → check inventory → flag lead times) without losing context - Local execution means data never leaves PGC's control

Bottom line: Agent memory unlocks real business process automation—not just chatbots, but systems that take action across your workflows.

3. Capital Pressure Creates Vendor Leverage

What it is: S&P 500's rejection of unprofitable AI firms (OpenAI, Anthropic) signals markets demanding proven ROI. AI vendors must now compete on reliability and enterprise contracts.

Why it matters for PGC: - Vendors are more willing to offer SLAs, price guarantees, and accountability provisions - Negotiation window is open NOW—vendors need wins - Self-hosted alternatives give PGC a credible fallback in negotiations

Bottom line: You're in the driver's seat. Use it.

4. Rust-Based AI Tooling Gains Traction

What it is: New AI tools built in Rust (Goose, Rift)—a language known for speed, safety, and low operational overhead. This contrasts with traditional Python-heavy stacks.

Why it matters for PGC: - Rust tools run faster and crash less—critical for real-time applications (e.g., design rendering, site estimating) - Lower maintenance burden over time - Signals a maturing ecosystem moving beyond prototyping

Bottom line: Next-generation AI tools will be faster and more reliable. Expect integration into glazing workflows to become practical within 6-12 months.

5. Multi-Modal AI Expands Beyond Text

What it is: AI that processes images, audio, and 3D geometry—not just text. Examples: TripoSplat (2D→3D conversion), JoyAI-Echo (audio-visual generation).

Why it matters for PGC: - Generate 3D glazing models from 2D architectural drawings - Create client-facing visualizations in minutes, not days - Potential to automate design reviews and specification checks

Bottom line: Multi-modal AI directly addresses glazing industry bottlenecks around visualization and client communication.

MVP Experiments (1-7 Days)

Run these low-cost experiments to test feasibility before committing resources:

🧪 MVP #1: Local Document Retrieval Test (1-2 Days)

Goal: Validate self-hosted AI for internal knowledge management

Step Action
1 Deploy Odysseus locally on a spare machine
2 Load 10-20 past project specs, client briefs, and pricing sheets
3 Ask the AI: "What glazing specs did we use for the Meridian Tower project?"
4 Measure retrieval accuracy and time vs. manual search

Success criteria: Answers are accurate within 24 hours. No data leaves your network.

Estimated effort: 1-2 days. Estimated cost: $0 (Odysseus is open-source).

🧪 MVP #2: AI Vendor Benchmarking (2-3 Days)

Goal: Establish negotiating baseline and compare costs

Step Action
1 Document PGC's hard requirements: data security, uptime, cost ceiling
2 Request proposals from 3 AI vendors (cloud and hybrid)
3 Compare total cost of ownership vs. self-hosted Odysseus + Memory-OS
4 Ask vendors: "What SLA can you guarantee? What happens if you miss it?"

Success criteria: You have apples-to-apples comparisons and leverage for negotiation.

Estimated effort: 2-3 days. Estimated cost: Staff time only.

🧪 MVP #3: 2D→3D Visualization Proof-of-Concept (3-5 Days)

Goal: Test AI-generated glazing renderings from architectural drawings

Step Action
1 Find a public 2D floor plan or elevation drawing from a past project
2 Feed it into TripoSplat or similar 2D→3D tool
3 Manually add glazing layer (windows, curtain walls) in a 3D tool
4 Assess: fidelity, time-to-result, client-facing quality

Success criteria: Visualization is good enough to show a client (even if not production-ready).

Estimated effort: 3-5 days. Estimated cost: $0 (many tools have free tiers).

Strategic Implications for PGC

Near-Term (0-90 Days)

  1. Start with MVP #1 (Local Document Retrieval). Prove value with low-risk experiment. Measure time savings on project spec retrieval.

  2. Initiate MVP #2 (Vendor Benchmarking). Establish negotiating baseline before committing to any cloud AI vendor.

  3. Prioritize self-hosted architecture. Local-first minimizes risk, data exposure, and vendor lock-in. The tooling is now stable enough.

Medium-Term (90-180 Days)

  1. Deploy AI proposal generation (MVP #2 from original research). Based on learnings from document retrieval test.

  2. Explore design visualization (MVP #3). If resources allow, test 2D→3D rendering for client-facing mockups.
